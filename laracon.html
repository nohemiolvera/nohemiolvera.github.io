Laracon US 2026: The cube in motion.

1 min read

Client:
Laravel
Role:
Design lead

The visual identity for Laracon US 2026, Laravel’s flagship conference in Boston: one shape, the cube, caught at every stage of becoming.

Scope

  • Event identity
  • Cube generator tool
  • Stage, speaker, and livestream graphics
  • Merch, wayfinding, registration, and badges

Outcomes

  • +950 attendees in Boston
  • 25,000 livestream viewers
  • One identity across every touchpoint, from the main stage to a badge
  • Cube generator let the team produce on-brand assets on demand, in SVG or PNG at any size

The idea

Every app starts as an outline. By the time you ship, it has weight.

Taylor Otwell, Laravel’s founder and CEO, built it because he had an idea he wanted to ship. Not a framework for millions, just something specific he wanted to make real. That’s still the mission: given the right tools, anyone can become a shipper.

That barrier has never been lower. People with no development background are shipping web apps. The people Laravel was built for just got a lot bigger, and Laracon US 2026 is where they all come together.

An idea has no weight, no color, no presence: just the shape of something that could exist. Then you build, and it gets color, dimension, and solidity. By the time you ship, you’ve made something that wasn’t there before. That’s every app ever built on Laravel, and every person coming to Boston.

Apps begin as outlines and gain weight as they’re built and shipped.

So instead of one fixed mark, the identity catches the cube at every stage of that journey: a small square, the lines that pull it forward, and the solid shape it becomes.

The cube generator

A tool for on-brand assets, without waiting on design.

The identity needed new cube compositions all the time, more than design could draw one by one. So I built a generator: the team picks a grid or radial layout, adjusts the cubes, and exports an SVG or PNG at any size.

Every asset stays on system, and no one has to wait on a design request to get one.

At the event

Readable from the back row and from a foot away.

The identity had to work at every scale at once: a stage screen read from the back row, a speaker slide on the livestream, and a badge read from a foot away. The same parts carried every touchpoint: stage graphics, speaker introductions, the livestream, merch, wayfinding, registration, and badges. Bold condensed type and Laracon orange did the shouting, and the cube motifs held it all together.
