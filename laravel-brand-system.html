Laravel brand system: Built so marketing can move faster.

3 min read

Client:
Laravel
Role:
Digital designer

Six teams were sending 6–8 requests a day with no shared intake. I moved the repeated ones and their revision rounds to self-serve tools and the Claude brand system, freeing around 15 hours of design time a week.

Scope

Led end to end: discovery, system, tooling, rollout

Outcomes

  • 6–8 requests a day → now handled by marketing, no designer needed, around 15 hours a week of design time freed
  • Self-serve tools and design system used daily by demand gen, blog, and social
  • One Figma source powers both the human guidelines site and the Claude brand system

The approach

Instead of solving one-offs, I set one rule: repeated needs become self-serve tools or AI skills. High-craft work stays with design.

Discovery Conversations with the marketing team, a review of recent requests to see what repeated, and how people were already using AI for design.

The goal Marketing moves faster, and design spends its time on the work only design can do.

Not everything gets automated Some needs fit a tool or a template, and some still need a designer’s eye. Automating those would only have made them faster and worse.

Discovery

Every request is a signal. I mapped what each team needed, then sent each need where it fits.

The system

One source, three layers, every output. A change made once in Figma reaches everything people and agents make.

1. Source of truth. Color, type, spacing, logos, and illustration, defined once in Figma.

2. The brand, written down. Rules in plain words that people and Claude both follow, checks that catch anything drifting from the source, and a log of every decision with its reason. A fix made once carries into every future asset instead of staying in one project.

3. What people use. The guidelines site, the Claude brand system, and the self-serve tools, each inheriting every brand decision from the layers above.

What shipped

What I learned

AI sped up the process. Knowing the team shaped what we built.

I knew the people on both sides: the audience the assets speak to, and the marketing team who makes them.

Working closely with the team, I came to understand their pain points, recognized the patterns in what they needed, and built tools that help them move faster. That’s why the illustrations are labeled by concept, and why type and color were locked before anything else. Those are design decisions, not defaults.

What’s next

The image tool launched with open-source illustrations (Isocons) as a deliberate MVP stand-in, to lock type and color first.

Next step: a custom illustration library labeled by concept, replacing the placeholder set.

Results

Fewer design requests, and the right owner for every change.

Title and copy changes moved to the people who own the copy, so late edits no longer reopen a design request.

Tracked through three signals: fewer repeat requests, steady daily use, and fewer off-brand assets needing fixes.
